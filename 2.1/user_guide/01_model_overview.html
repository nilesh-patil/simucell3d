Model overview#

SimuCell3D is a deformable cell model (DCM) in three dimensions. Every cell of a tissue is a closed, triangulated surface that deforms under its own mechanical energy and under contact with other cells. This chapter describes what a simulated tissue consists of, what the solver does in one iteration, which choices are made at compile time and which in the parameter file, and how the 2.1 code relates to the model published by Runser, Vetter & Iber (2024). The following chapters treat each part in detail.

A tissue as a set of closed surfaces#

A cell is represented only by its surface: a closed, orientable triangle mesh made of nodes, edges and faces. The faces are wound so that their normals point out of the cell. The interior is not discretised; the cell volume \(V\) and area \(A\) are computed from the surface (chapter Cell mechanics). The forces of the model act on the nodes, and the nodes carry the degrees of freedom: a position \(\mathbf r_i\) and, with the full equations of motion, a momentum \(\mathbf p_i\).

Three levels of description give each part of a cell its parameters:

Cell type

An entry <cell_type> of the parameter file. It holds the volume and area parameters (bulk modulus, target isoperimetric ratio, growth and division parameters, …) and a list of face types. Each cell of the input mesh names its cell type by position in the file (chapter Cell classes).

Cell class

The C++ class that implements a cell type, selected by global_cell_id: 0 epithelial, 1 extracellular matrix (ECM), 2 lumen, 3 nucleus, 4 static. The class decides whether a cell moves, divides, couples to neighbours and has its faces polarized.

Face type

An entry <face_type> inside a cell type. It holds the surface parameters: surface tension, bending modulus, adherence strength and repulsion strength. Every face of a cell carries one face type of its cell type. For epithelial cells the face types stand for apical, lateral and basal membrane, and the solver can relabel faces as contacts change (chapter Polarization).

Lumens, nuclei and the ECM are represented the same way as cells: by closed triangulated surfaces with their own cell type. They differ from epithelial cells only in the behaviour of their class.

Forces and motion#

The mechanical state of a cell is described by the potential energy of Runser, Vetter & Iber (2024), Eq. (1): a pressure term that keeps the volume near a target volume, an area elasticity term, surface tension, and bending. Its negative gradient gives the internal nodal forces, except that the implemented bending force is not an exact gradient (chapter Cell mechanics). Contact forces between cells come from one of three contact models: springs between nodes and faces, or a mechanical coupling of nodes on adjacent cells (chapter Contact between cells). The nodes then move according to either the full equations of motion with viscous damping or their overdamped limit (chapter Time integration).

Between force evaluations the local mesh refiner keeps every edge length within \([l_{\min}, 3\,l_{\min}]\) (chapter Mesh refinement). Cells grow by increasing their target volume, divide when their volume reaches a division volume, and are removed when their volume falls below a minimum (chapter Growth, division and removal).

One iteration of the solver#

The solver advances the tissue by one time step \(\Delta t\) per iteration. In 2.1 one iteration performs these steps, in this order:

 1. write the cell and face meshes if a new sampling period has started
 2. every 5 iterations: divide the cells that are ready to divide
 3. reset the face types of epithelial cells (all faces apical)
 4. refine the mesh of every cell (split, collapse, swap); remove cells whose refinement fails
 5. detect contacts and compute contact forces (or node couplings)
 6. polarization mode 2 only: label faces from a voxel map of the tissue
 7. polarization mode 1 with contact models 1 and 2: relabel faces from their couplings;
    compute the internal forces of every cell (pressure, tension, area elasticity, bending)
 8. integrate the equations of motion; advance the simulation time by the time step
 9. every 50 iterations: append to the statistics CSV
    every 100 iterations: append to the diagnostics CSV
10. remove cells below their minimum volume and numerically unstable cells

Most steps run in parallel over cells with OpenMP. The time integration with contact model 2 and the voxel map of polarization mode 2 are serial. The run stops when the simulation time reaches simulation_duration or when no cell is left.

Changed in version 2.1: Step 4 removes a cell whose mesh refinement throws an error instead of ending the run, and step 10 also removes numerically unstable cells. Both are safeguards; see Safeguards.

Some model choices are fixed when SimuCell3D is compiled; the others are read from the parameter file at start-up.

Every shipped parameter file states in its header comment which compile-time configuration it was written for. Chapter Compile-time configuration explains how to change the configuration and rebuild.

Units#

All quantities are in SI units: lengths in m, times in s, pressures in Pa, surface tensions in N/m, energies in J. The parameter files store plain numbers; the unit of each tag is given in Parameter file reference. Simulation time is the physical time of the equations of motion; it is not rescaled.

Relationship to the published model#

The model and its numerical methods are described in the Methods section of Runser, Vetter & Iber (2024) and in its Supplementary Information. SimuCell3D 2.1 implements that model with the differences listed in the notes of the following chapters. The main ones are:

  • The default contact model in the code, face-face coupling (contact model 2), is not one of the two models described in the paper’s Methods; it extends the node-node coupling of the paper so that a node can be coupled to one node on each neighbouring cell. It is the default of the tag v1.0 and of 2.x, but not of the Zenodo archive cited by the paper, whose default is contact model 1 (Release 1.0).

  • The target area is computed from the current volume, not from the target volume (chapter Cell mechanics).

  • The bending force has a prefactor of 3 where the paper’s Methods give 2. The code’s 3 matches Eq. (1); the Methods’ discrete bending energy, and the bending_energy written to the output, are about 2/3 of it. The implemented bending force is also not an exact gradient of the bending energy, a defect inherited from 1.0 (chapter Cell mechanics).

  • The division plane is always normal to the longest cell axis; there is no random orientation (chapter Growth, division and removal).

Important

Results differ from 1.0. A simulation run with SimuCell3D 2.1 does not reproduce the numbers produced by 1.0, the code published with the paper. Several 1.0 bugs changed the physics (the contact distance to a face interior, the time step of every node with contact model 2, the choice of coupling partner, the face orientation after an edge split), and fixing them changes trajectories. 2.1 also adds safeguards that remove or repair cells that 1.0 kept or crashed on. To compare with the published figures, run release 1.0. The full list is in Release 2.1.
