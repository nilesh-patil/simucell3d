Polarization#

Epithelial cells have membrane regions with different mechanical properties: an apical side facing a lumen or the outside, lateral sides in contact with neighbouring cells, and a basal side facing the extracellular matrix. SimuCell3D represents these regions by face types, each with its own surface tension, bending modulus, adherence strength and repulsion strength. This chapter explains how faces receive their types and how the polarization mode (POLARIZATION_MODE_INDEX, a compile-time choice) relabels them during a run.

Face types#

Each <cell_type> of the parameter file lists one or more <face_type> entries. Inside a cell, a face refers to its face type by position in that list: the first entry is local face type 0, the second 1, the third 2. The tag global_face_id is the number written to the output meshes as face_type_id, so that face types of different cell types can be told apart in ParaView; it does not select the face type.

The polarization code assigns local types to the faces of epithelial cells with this meaning:

Local face type

Region

0

apical

1

lateral

2

basal

The face types of an epithelial cell type must therefore be listed in the order apical, lateral, basal, as in every shipped parameter file:

<face_types>
    <face_type>
        <global_face_id>0</global_face_id>
        <face_type_name>apical</face_type_name>
        <adherence_strength>1e9</adherence_strength>
        <repulsion_strength>1e9</repulsion_strength>
        <surface_tension>1e-3</surface_tension>
        <bending_modulus>0</bending_modulus>
    </face_type>
    <face_type>
        <global_face_id>1</global_face_id>
        <face_type_name>lateral</face_type_name>
        <adherence_strength>1e9</adherence_strength>
        <repulsion_strength>1e9</repulsion_strength>
        <surface_tension>4e-4</surface_tension>
        <bending_modulus>0</bending_modulus>
    </face_type>
    <face_type>
        <global_face_id>2</global_face_id>
        <face_type_name>basal</face_type_name>
        <adherence_strength>0</adherence_strength>
        <repulsion_strength>5e8</repulsion_strength>
        <surface_tension>1e-3</surface_tension>
        <bending_modulus>0</bending_modulus>
    </face_type>
</face_types>

(from parameters/core/parameters_vesicle.xml). The input mesh carries no face types: every face starts with local type 0. Faces created by an edge split copy the type of the face they replace. Only epithelial cells are relabelled; the faces of lumen, ECM, nucleus and static cells keep local type 0, so these cell types need only one face type.

Polarization modes#

In every iteration, after the division check and before mesh refinement, all faces of every epithelial cell are reset to local type 0 (apical), whatever the polarization mode. The polarization mode then decides which faces become lateral or basal in the same iteration, before the internal forces are computed.

Mode 0: no polarization#

No face is relabelled. All faces of epithelial cells use the first face type for the whole run.

Mode 1: from contacts (default)#

Faces are labelled from the contacts found by the contact model. The rule depends on the contact model:

  • Contact model 0. When a node of another cell is in adhesive or repulsive contact with a face of an epithelial cell, the face is labelled from the cell class of the touching cell: ECM gives basal (2), lumen gives apical (0), any other class gives lateral (1).

  • Contact model 2. A face is lateral (1) if its three nodes are all coupled to nodes of the same neighbouring cell and the three partner nodes form a face of that cell. All other faces are apical (0).

  • Contact model 1. A face whose three nodes are all coupled, and at least one of whose node normals points to the same side as the face normal, is lateral (1) if the partners lie on different cells or are joined by edges on the same cell, and apical (0) otherwise. Faces with an uncoupled node stay apical.

No face becomes basal with contact models 1 and 2, because couplings exist only between epithelial cells.

With contact models 1 and 2, mode 1 therefore distinguishes only contact with another epithelial cell (lateral) from everything else (apical); the basal face type is not used.

Mode 2: from a voxel map of the tissue#

This mode follows the second algorithm of Runser, Vetter & Iber (2024), Fig. 2, and works without contact information. At every iteration:

  1. The bounding box of the tissue, enlarged by two voxels on each side, is divided into cubic voxels of edge \(3\,l_{\min}\).

  2. Every voxel that contains a mesh node is a boundary voxel.

  3. Starting from the voxel of each cell’s centroid, a flood fill over face-adjacent voxels marks the cytoplasm of every cell, stopping at boundary voxels.

  4. A flood fill from a corner of the box marks the exterior. (The paper names the Hoshen-Kopelman algorithm for this clustering; the code uses flood fills over the six face neighbours of each voxel.)

  5. All remaining voxels are luminal.

  6. For every face of an epithelial cell, a ray is cast from its first node along the outward face normal. The first non-boundary voxel it reaches gives the region the face looks at.

In 2.1 a face that looks at exterior space becomes local type 0, and a face that looks at cytoplasm or at a lumen becomes local type 1. The voxel map is written at every sampling period to region_data/result_<n>.vtk, from the third iteration on (Output files).

Note

The paper describes the outcome as lateral (facing another cell), apical (facing an enclosed volume such as a lumen) and basal (facing the surrounding medium or ECM). The 2.1 code, unchanged from 1.0, maps lumen and cytoplasm to the same local type 1 and the exterior to local type 0, and never assigns local type 2. Check the face_type_id array of the face meshes before relying on mode 2 for apical-basal differences.

The cytoplasm flood fill starts at the centroid of the cell surface. For a strongly non-convex cell whose centroid lies outside the cell, the fill marks the wrong region. A ray that runs along a row of boundary voxels can also report the exterior for a face that is in contact with another cell.

Effect on the mechanics#

The face type of a face selects, at every iteration:

  • its surface tension \(\gamma\) and the bending modulus used for the hinges at its edges (Cell mechanics);

  • the adherence strength \(\omega\) and repulsion strength \(\xi\) applied when a node of another cell touches it (Contact between cells).

A lower lateral than apical surface tension, as in the shipped files, favours large contacts between cells: for two coupled cells, the angle \(\theta\) between each free surface and the contact plane at the rim follows the Young-Dupré relation \(\cos\theta = \gamma_l/\gamma_a\) (paper, Supplementary Fig. 5), because the interface carries the lateral tension of both cells. The paper explores the balance between surface tension and adhesion in its Figs. 3 and 4.

Contact area fraction#

With polarization mode 1, the statistics CSV has a column cell_contact_area_fraction: for an epithelial cell, the area of its lateral faces divided by its total area; for other cells, 0. The column is absent in the other modes (Output files).

Compile-time options#

POLARIZATION_MODE_INDEX

Behaviour

0

No relabelling; epithelial faces stay apical

1 (default)

From contacts, as described above

2

From the voxel map; writes region_data/

Every shipped parameter file states its configuration in its header, and all of them were written for mode 1. Changing the mode requires a rebuild (Compile-time configuration).
