Growth, division and cell death#

The number and size of cells change during a run. Cells grow by increasing their target volume, epithelial cells divide when they reach a division volume, and cells die when their volume falls below a minimum (a minimum-volume death, part of the model). This chapter describes each rule as implemented in 2.1, with its parameters, and the divisions that 2.1 refuses.

Growth#

Growth acts on the target volume \(V_0\), not on the volume directly. At every iteration, before the pressure is computed,

(1)#\[ V_0 \leftarrow \max\left(V_0 + g\, \Delta t,\ V_{\min}\right), \]

which is the linear growth law \(\mathrm dV_0/\mathrm dt = g\) of the paper’s Methods, with \(V_{\min}\) (min_vol) as a lower bound. The cell’s volume then follows \(V_0\) through the pressure term (Cell mechanics): a cell whose volume lags behind its target volume has a positive pressure and expands. A negative \(g\) shrinks the cell. Growth applies to every cell class whose internal forces are computed, that is all classes except the ECM; the shipped files give growth only to epithelial cells.

The growth rate \(g\) is a property of each cell. It is drawn when the cell is created (at start-up or at its birth by division) from a normal distribution with mean avg_growth_rate and standard deviation std_growth_rate, clipped to the mean \(\pm 3\) standard deviations. With a standard deviation of 0 every cell gets the mean. The growth rate is in m³ per second of simulation time; the shipped files do not define a conversion to biological time.

Division#

Only epithelial cells divide. Every 5 iterations, each epithelial cell whose current volume \(V\) has reached its division volume \(V_\mathrm{div}\) is divided. Like the growth rate, \(V_\mathrm{div}\) is drawn per cell from a normal distribution (avg_division_volume, std_division_volume), clipped to \(\pm 3\) standard deviations; if the standard deviation is 0 or the mean is inf, the mean is used. avg_division_volume set to inf disables division.

The division of a mother cell proceeds as follows:

  1. Plane. The cutting plane passes through the area-weighted centroid of the surface and is normal to the cell’s longest axis: the eigenvector with the largest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of the node positions. This is Hertwig’s rule.

  2. Cut. The points where the plane crosses the edges of the mesh are inserted, and the faces they split are re-triangulated.

  3. Interface. The outline of the cut is a polygon in the plane. It is filled with points by Poisson disk sampling at spacing \(l_{\min}\) and triangulated with a two-dimensional Delaunay triangulation, so that the new wall respects the edge-length bounds and the rest of the surface is kept from the mother.

  4. Daughters. The surface is split into two closed meshes, one on each side of the cut, each closed by the triangulated interface. Both are refined by the local mesh refiner.

  5. Properties. Each daughter receives half the target volume of the mother, a new identifier, and newly drawn growth rate and division volume. The daughters have the cell type of the mother.

The mother is removed and the two daughters are added to the tissue. Runser, Vetter & Iber (2024) report that a division takes a few microseconds of computation time.

Note

The paper’s Methods allow a randomly oriented division plane as an alternative. The 2.1 code has only the longest-axis plane.

Cuts with several loops#

If the plane also crosses a thin protrusion of the cell, its intersection with the surface consists of more than one closed loop.

Changed in version 2.1: Every loop is traced, the cell is cut along the longest one, and the two daughters are separated by a flood fill over the surface that does not cross the cut. In 1.0 only one loop was followed, the division failed, and the cell tried again at every division check (every 5 iterations).

Refused divisions#

The two-dimensional Delaunay triangulation is not constrained by the outline of the interface. If the outline is not convex, some of its sides can be missing from the triangulation, which would leave a hole between the interface and the rest of each daughter.

Changed in version 2.1: Such a division is refused: the mother is left unchanged and divides at a later division check, with new random interior points. The log line is

Cell division failed for cell <id>: Interface outline not covered by the Delaunay triangulation (<k> of <n> sides missing); division refused

An earlier 2.1 development version patched these interfaces instead; the refiner could not repair the resulting daughters, which were then removed as unstable.

Refusals become more frequent as the tissue grows. In an 8-core run of the reconstructed paper-like configuration (parameters/longrun_fast_growth.xml, not the lab’s configuration; Tissue growth from a single cell) from 1 to 150,498 cells, 14% of division attempts were refused (5% below 30,000 cells, 21% between 125,000 and 150,000 cells); a refused mother was refused 1.75 times on average, at most 31 times. A cell with a fixed shape can be refused every time until its shape changes. Refused divisions therefore delay some divisions; they do not lose cells. The CHANGELOG lists them as a known issue. The lab’s growth benchmark (contact model 1) refuses more: in the November 2026 benchmark, 2.1.0 refused about 21% of division attempts below 30,000 cells and 27% between 125,000 and 150,000 cells, about 170,000 refusals in a 48-hour run (Limits of the model).

The two-dimensional Delaunay step itself has no iteration limit: its edge-flipping loop runs until the triangulation is legal. Two intermediate development builds hung there in the November 2026 benchmark; no 2.1.0 run did, but the code is unchanged. A run that stops printing progress lines while a division check is due may be stuck in this loop.

Other errors during a division (for example a cut that does not split the surface in two) are logged with the same Cell division failed for cell <id>: prefix, and the mother also retries at the next division check.

Cell death at the minimum volume#

At the end of every iteration, a cell is deleted if its volume is below its minimum volume:

\[ V < V_{\min}. \]

This minimum-volume death models cell death (apoptosis) in the paper. It applies to every cell class and is not logged: no log line, no counter, and no column of the diagnostics CSV records it. Because the target volume never falls below \(V_{\min}\) ((1)), shrinkage alone brings a cell only down to about \(V_{\min}\); cells die when their neighbours compress them.

These deaths can be numerous. In the lab’s growth benchmark, measured with 2.1.0 in November 2026, 360,009 cells had died by the time 144,000 were alive, 36% of all cells ever born (Limits of the model). To count them, use the cell_id column of the statistics CSV: identifiers are given in sequence from 0, so \(N_\mathrm{created}\), the largest identifier plus one, counts every cell ever created, and each division adds two cells, so there were \((N_\mathrm{created} - N_\mathrm{initial})/2\) divisions. The deaths are then \(N_\mathrm{created} - N_\mathrm{alive} - N_\mathrm{divisions} - N_\mathrm{safeguard}\), where \(N_\mathrm{safeguard}\) counts the safeguard removals in the log (Safeguards).

Removal of unstable cells#

Changed in version 2.1: The same step also removes numerically unstable cells, and a cell whose mesh refinement fails is removed earlier in the iteration. These removals are safeguards, not part of the published model, and unlike deaths they are logged; see Safeguards.

The paper’s Table 2 gives \(g = 10^{-11}\) m³/s, \(V_\mathrm{div} = 1.4\times10^{-15}\) m³ (called \(V_{\max}\) there) and \(V_{\min} = 3.7\times10^{-16}\) m³; parameters/longrun_fast_growth.xml uses these values with standard deviations of 10% of the means.

Practical guidance#

  • Growth rate and time step. The target volume grows by \(g\Delta t\) per iteration. With the Table 2 values, a cell takes \((V_\mathrm{div} - V_\mathrm{div}/2)/g = 7\times10^{-5}\) s, or 700 iterations at a time step of 1e-7 s, from birth to division. The paper describes this rate as “pushed to the limit” for its growth benchmark; the volume can lag behind the target volume at such rates.

  • Random draws. The generators are seeded from the wall clock, so with non-zero standard deviations two runs never draw the same values. The point sampling that triangulates the division interface is also seeded from the clock, so runs in which cells divide differ even with standard deviations of 0 and one thread. There is no parameter for a fixed seed, so a growth run cannot be repeated bit for bit. In the November 2026 benchmark, repeats of the growth benchmark differed from the first iteration on, while there was still one cell, because its initial triangulation is sampled the same way (Reproducibility).

  • Synchronous divisions. With standard deviations of 0, cells born together grow at the same rate and tend to divide at the same division check, which produces bursts of divisions and of mesh operations.

  • Counting divisions. The divisions column of the diagnostics CSV counts divisions per 100 iterations. Refusals and other division failures appear only in the log (Safeguards).
